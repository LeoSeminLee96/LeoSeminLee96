# Hi, I'm Leo 👋

I'm a **software engineer and aspiring cyber-physical systems researcher** interested in **robotics, intelligent physical systems, disassembly, remanufacturing, and industrial automation**.

My background is primarily in **software development, computer science, and artificial intelligence**. Through building and studying software systems, I also developed a strong interest in **cybersecurity**, particularly in understanding how systems fail, how vulnerabilities emerge from implementation and architectural decisions, and how systems can be designed more securely.

I am now expanding this engineering background toward systems that operate directly in the physical world, particularly **robotic manipulation, disassembly, remanufacturing, inspection, and maintenance-oriented automation**.

My main research interest is in **Cyber-Physical Systems (CPS)** — systems where software, sensing, AI, networking, control, robotics, and physical processes interact.

I am particularly interested in questions such as:

- How can robots understand the state and structure of physical objects?
- How can machines reason about disassembly, inspection, maintenance, and remanufacturing?
- How can AI support planning and decision-making in uncertain physical environments?
- How can software and physical components interact reliably in complex systems?
- How can cyber-physical systems remain safe, secure, robust, and verifiable?

For me, cybersecurity is closely connected to **software and systems engineering**. My interest in security grew from developing software and wanting to understand not only how systems are built, but also **how they fail, what assumptions can be violated, and how those failures should influence system design**.

As software increasingly controls physical machines, robots, sensors, and industrial equipment, I see security as one of several essential engineering considerations alongside **safety, reliability, fault tolerance, and system integrity**.

## 🧭 How I Approach Engineering

I tend to approach technical systems through the following cycle:

> **Build → Observe → Understand → Diagnose → Reason → Act → Verify → Improve**

Building systems leads naturally to questions about their internal behavior, limitations, failure modes, reliability, and security.

For cyber-physical systems, this perspective extends from software into the physical environment:

> **Perceive → Diagnose → Plan → Manipulate → Inspect → Verify → Adapt**

This intersection between **software, intelligence, and physical action** is the direction I intend to explore more deeply.
